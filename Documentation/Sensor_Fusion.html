Input data we record per session

Every recorded session writes up to four CSV/WAV files to the SD card. The sensors and their roles:

Source Rate What it gives us Used in fusion?
LSM6DSV16X — 3D gyroscope 100 Hz Angular velocity X/Y/Z, ±500 dps full scale, 17.5 mdps/LSB Yes — Madgwick prediction step
LSM6DSV16X — 3D accelerometer 100 Hz Specific force X/Y/Z, ±4 g full scale, 0.122 mg/LSB Yes — Madgwick correction step
LIS2MDL — 3D magnetometer (compass) 100 Hz Magnetic field X/Y/Z (mgauss). Offset cancellation + temp comp + LPF on. No — iron-distorted, see below
LPS22DF — barometer 100 Hz Pressure (hPa) and temperature. Used for board height above water. No — downstream height pipeline only
u-blox MAX-M10S — GPS position 10 Hz Lat/lon/alt + UTC time, NMEA GGA + RMC No — ride detection, map, height anchor
u-blox MAX-M10S — GPS speed 10 Hz Position-derived (haversine), not module Doppler-speed (unreliable) No — ride detection + push-off trigger

The fusion filter

We use a 6DOF Madgwick AHRS filter — gyro + accel only. Implementation: Utilities/rust/stbox-viz/src/fusion.rs (port of the classic Madgwick 2010 IMU variant). Magnetometer is deliberately excluded; the other four signals are used downstream of fusion, not as inputs to it.

Why we drop the compass

Validation against GPS course-over-ground (Ermioni, 22.4.2026):

  • median residual: −14°
  • p5–p95 spread: 307° — essentially random

The LIS2MDL is hard/soft-iron-distorted by ferromagnetic mounting hardware. Feeding it into a 9DOF gradient would couple heading garbage into roll & pitch and corrupt the whole quaternion. So we accept that yaw has no absolute reference. The fix is hardware (plastic screws, redesigned enclosure away from steel mounts).

The math, per sample

  1. Predict the quaternion derivative from the gyroscope alone: q̇ = ½ · q ⊗ ω.
  2. Compute the Madgwick gradient of the accelerometer error (the rotated world gravity vector should match the measured body-frame accel).
  3. Subtract β · ∇F / ‖∇F‖ from the gyro-predicted derivative.
  4. Integrate by dt, then renormalise the quaternion.

β is the tuning knob — how strongly we trust accel vs gyro. dt is auto-detected from the median tick delta in the CSV (1 tick = 10 ms ⇒ 100 Hz ⇒ dt = 0.01 s).

Input units into Madgwick

  • Accel: mg. Direction-only after normalisation.
  • Gyro: mdps. Converted to rad/s before integration.

What downstream code does with each signal

Quaternion → Nasenwinkel

nose_angle_series_deg projects the body-Y axis (board nose direction) into world Z and takes asin:

nose_z  = 2 · (qj·qk − qs·qi)
angle° = asin(nose_z)

Drift correction: 1 s centred rolling median (suppresses correction spikes) followed by subtraction of a 60 s rolling-median baseline. This drives the Nasenwinkel trace and the pump-cadence STFT.

Quaternion → 3D foil rendering

Mast-mount (animate --mount mast): orientation straight from the Madgwick quaternion with quat_strip_yaw removing the drifting yaw. Captures the 90° tail-place transient just before push-off because Madgwick integrates the gyro continuously.

Deck-mount (animate --mount deck): bypasses Madgwick and uses accel-only tilt — 0.25 s rolling mean on accel, then pitch = atan2(−ay, √(ax²+az²)), roll = atan2(ax, az). Reason: gyro-Z bias survives quat_strip_yaw once the mast is tilted, making the rendered foil spin around its own mast on clean rides.

Barometer → height above water

Two-stage correction (baro.rs):

  1. Temperature compensation. The LPS22DF sits in a semi-sealed enclosure, so pressure couples to temperature via the ideal-gas law. A 10 °C swing produces ~15 hPa of fake altitude — far larger than the 0.08 hPa signal from the 80 cm mast.
  2. GPS-anchored water reference. When GPS speed < 3 km/h the board is in the water; we linear-interpolate the TC'd pressure across flying segments and compute height via the local hypsometric approximation 8434 · (1 − P/Pref).

Final guards: clamp to physical mast range [−0.1, 0.9] m, NaN samples where 10 ms pressure delta exceeds 1 hPa (sensor glitches). NaN-aware smoothing keeps neighbouring real samples valid.

GPS → ride detection, map, push-off

  • Ride detection. Sustained GPS speed > 3 km/h for ≥ 10 s, merge gaps < 30 s, pad ± 3 s. More reliable than the pitch-oscillation detector for smooth flying where the board barely pitches.
  • Map trace. One Plotly trace per ride, coloured by speed (Viridis 0–25 km/h), hover shows UTC + speed + nose angle + height-above-water.
  • Push-off trigger. First sample where a 1 s rolling mean of raw position-derived speed exceeds 4 km/h sustainedly for 0.5 s. Drives the phase-band shading (Tragen / Rennen / Foilen) and the Push-off-Winkel flash.
  • Speed cleaning. Two-stage filter: reject samples with |Δv|/Δt > 15 km/h/s (multipath spikes), then 5 s rolling median. Module Doppler-speed is unreliable on this hardware (median 0.12 km/h while position deltas show 10–30 km/h), so we always derive speed from position.
The pump-cadence trace is still driven by the Madgwick output even in deck-mount mode — only the 3D mesh path uses the accel-only attitude. That keeps the pump fundamental (~0.5 Hz) clean while giving us a stable visual rendering.

Carry phase — explicitly hidden

During the carry phase (no GPS speed) the 3D mesh is hidden completely — the panel shows “Tragen — keine 3D-Daten”. Without an absolute heading reference (no usable mag, GPS course unreliable below ~3 km/h) any rendered orientation during carry is a guess; we'd rather show no foil than a wrong one. The 3D rendering switches on at water_set_t (sustained GPS speed > 4 km/h) or after a 10.5 s fallback timeout.
